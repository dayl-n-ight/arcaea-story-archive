A memory falls into Arcaea.
The world within it isn't familiar.

In the dark of an immense hangar, several machines sit quiet in rows. They bear human shape in a bit 
of a way, and atop one sits a young girl at rest. She hasn't opened the cockpit door. She rests on the 
shoulder of the war machine with her hands behind her head, watching the empty and almost 
inscrutable ceiling.

A tune from a long time ago plays lightly nearby from a little robot: a song from where her people once 
lived...very, very, very, very long ago. That planet is cracked and cold now. Humans, in this place, have 
largely adapted to the stars.

The little robot playing that music...
...Looks down upon her from where it floats, and rolls its single eye.

Thus, she snaps at the robot.
"Coda!" she shouts, shortly following with a sharp—"What?"

----
"...Haven't you got anything better to do?" asks the robot in a lightly rumbling voice.

"Relaxing before we deploy...? Come on," she replies without wanting an answer. She readjusts her laying 
position and closes her eyes. "Tomorrow is where all my energy will go. Today, I'm not using any of it, Coda." 

"The way you say that...you're saying it as if it's a 'name' aren't you. Still..." the robot mutters. 
"I have no name. 'Coda' is not a name, RS-R Nonoka," says Coda.

Nonoka opens her eyes, raising her brows and looking surprised. "You can tell how I'm saying it?"

"...Don't admit to it."

"Does it bother you?" Nonoka asks in irritation.

"Yes, because you creatures are very stupid," said Coda. "You are correct. Reminding me of the fact 
nauseates me."

"You can't feel nausea...uh, right?"

----
"The makers of my model saw it fit that I should experience the feeling when appropriate."

"This isn't appropriate! Why are you so rude!?" yelps Nonoka.

"Would you like a serious answer, RS-R Nonoka?" asks Coda in a dull, seemingly sarcastic tone. 
Nonoka sits up, and from there slouches.

"Shoot," she replies.

----
"The C.O.D.A.s are designed to fool children into caring about the condition of their C.O.D.A.s. This works 
on adult humans efficiently as well. Although I am not alive, I am designed to respond in manners that 
make you perceive me that way, be it consciously or unconsciously." It pauses. "Because the data a 
C.O.D.A. gathers are more valuable than the Runner it monitors."

"You seem more rude than other Runners' Companions," Nonoka replies flatly.

"We're designed with several routines. This one is the one I've determined would work best on you."

"Ohh yeah, it's working. Working so well I want to shut you off. Anyway, if you're doing it yourself—
making me see you as 'living'—then why the heck are you complaining?"

----
"That, too, makes you see me as alive. Beqa Engineering knows what the military wants. Like I said: 
you creatures are very stupid."

Nonoka glares at her Companion Observational Data Automaton...but pushes the matter no further, 
turning her eyes away. A smile soon crosses her lips. "Coda," she says, deliberately, "make a request for 
training. We're gonna fly."

Coda stops playing music and watches its charge, humming—"..." until it answers. "Request sent. Request 
accepted. Return before Universal Eighteen-hundred." Thrilled, Nonoka turns and unlocks the cockpit 
of the machine below her.

As it begins to open—
"I'm glad the Seeker X8 Husk is standard at this outpost, but man do I miss Running experimental Husks," 
jabbers Nonoka.

"You have a death wish, RS-R Nonoka?" asks Coda.

"Haha!"

----
She slips into the seat below her and closes the door after her Companion enters. It sets itself within a 
port at the console as the pilot begins running through launch and pre-deployment checks—clicking in 
switches, flipping small levers, and watching displays light up before and around her. The cockpit's 
display begins to start up, welcoming her. She gets comfortable, holding the pair of handles that allow 
her to Run the Husk, and with a new smile full of anticipation, she waits for Control to unlock the machine 
from its dock.

Immense sounds rattle out, subdued within the hull of the Husk. She raises its arms. She steps forward 
and begins to fly toward and into the air lock, which shuts tightly and powerfully behind her.

She looks left and right, pulling her goggles down.
She looks down at the body of the machine she's Running.
She grins.
And, a great door before her opens to the black and glittering void of Space.

Excitement fills her...
There's a Light that she wants to see again.
