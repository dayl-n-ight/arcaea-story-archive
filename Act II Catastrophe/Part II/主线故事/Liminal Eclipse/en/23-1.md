It isn't usual, you know.
Those bodies there in that poorly-made place...they aren't too poorly-made themselves.
That may be from the creator's wish for life—

At any rate, when that girl fell apart into threads of glass, that was no usual thing.

In order to make sure that she remained "herself" from one world to the next...

{{ruby|Arcaea's visitor|I n s i g h t}} reduced "Maya" to her basic parts...she wasn't destroyed.

Listen. Are you worried that a girl made of glass, upon leaving Arcaea, will always split or shatter
upon reaching the world's boundary? It is deeply unlikely.

Wishes made by gods aren't so fragile.

----
...Would you like to join that girl again? After she fell into a better place.

As a spiral of threads, she still was very conscious.

----
In a better place...
Marked by color, and known by sense...
Maya fell down from a black sky run through with rivers of amber.

A spiral of glass threads passed through a gate in that sky, and down into a quiet place.
She was sent gently through a doorway and, ungently, the door shut closed behind her.
She came back together on a vermillion carpet, and uncomfortably laid across stone:
the two-colored girl born of tragedy.

Crying again...

----
Her eyes opened to an unknown ceiling; her breathing quickened.
She wiped her tears away with her sleeves and,
"Who...Who was that..."
she whispered—to no answer.
Her eyes and mind flashed with the visitor's image.
"...What kind of...crazy...awful woman..." she muttered.

...

It seemed that although she had been saved, this girl was very hateful and ingracious.
Though, her swirl of thoughts should be kept to her head.
While Maya is rude, her thoughts should be hers.
To the curious: perhaps Arcaea will be rude once more
and record her thoughts without her knowing again.

----
Maya pushed herself up and rose to her knees.
She had heard the door lock behind her, but she tried it anyway.
It did not open, and she faced forward instead.
Another door waited ahead: a way further in, rather than out.

...After some time thinking, she stepped forward, and opened that door.
