...So Maya seemed to wish for her thoughts to be known? She has grown, it seems.

...So be it.

Maya did not only think of what had happened to her since her arrival to Arcaea,
she thought about what had come before.

She thought about the friends she had lost.

----
One memory:
A small, physical, book: out of fashion, yet Maya loved the tactile sensation—and she liked to be different.
At twelve years old, feeling unique even while in uniform was special to her.
She was proud of herself for many things, but this—reading a book
within her hands—was a particular point.
While reading, a friend of hers approached the chair she was resting in and asked—

"Where do you get those, anyway?"

A small shop, an older woman, and old things—remembering this...

She began to forget all of it. She picked herself up, then, and continued on through the world.

----
More days went on, more entries were entered into the book. At first, her history: from her first life,
to second, to capture. Her last few entries however concerned the mysteries
she had come across within the halls of God...

// Maya, 8. //

// I must begrudgingly and with great irritation accept at this point that magic is real.

Or, call it magic, call it miracles or power, call it even acts of "gods"—

Tricking pathways and almost-seen phantoms, lights that hang in the air from nothing, and this place's
impossible size. It might be growing. //

----
// —I can't easily believe in a life after.
It is a complete certainty that I died. I know what I feel, and I know that it was my fault.
Given this, and my death—

Given the new face and eyes which met me upon viewing my reflection—
it's a simple fact: I look very different now.
None of this is helped in that the memories which I wrote down to begin with
have begun fading from my head. //

----
// If the woman who trapped me was not a god as she claimed, was she instead a twisted angel?
Was she perhaps a reaper? Moronic.

Is this her home? Why does she carry no pictures or portraits with her own face?

And, if this is an afterlife then //

Her pen stopped. She looked up from her journal and through a small and circular window above her.

She thought...
If this truly is an "after" life...
If I did not die alone...

Would I ever be alone here? Well:
I didn't die alone, and my "voice" reaches beyond the stars.

----
...She was once military, once a "psychic".
Yes, she'd once shared a connection with many others—who have since died.

So, now...
Narrowing her eyes, she focused her gaze through the window above, and she called out
through that sky with her mind.

A hesitant message: "Is anyone there?"

...

----
And, there was a reply.

Another girl's reply, landing softly inside of her skull:

"I...am. Who... Who are you...?"
