On the other side...
The room was warm, and the quiet suited her ears.

A vast atrium. Above, the ceiling was ridged like the bones of a dead giant, and austere columns
large and domineering stood beside her.
Though feeling at ease, Maya looked at it all with clear skepticism.

She asked aloud: What kind of place was this, and where was the woman who had come from the sky?

Muttering about all that, she began to wander.

----
There's no dust in a perfect world, and no lack of order.

Everything that should have a place, has one. Maya would find that things from other places
are not dropped any "where" in this place, they are put down, warmly.

So, this place reflected the heart and love of God.

Statues and tomes...
Plants in rows, others in cases...
Globes, and other hanging or standing shapes—maps, of other places...
Tools and machines, displaying...
Fountains and mirrors...

A lot of things, and certainly not from one place.

----
As Maya's footsteps rang out long through the new room, she announced: "A manor...? ...It's empty?"

A standard voice. A test. But, it seemed that there was nobody there to hear.

Vast portraits, paintings—representations were hanging on half the walls she could find.
More baubles unfamiliar to her were in every hall and room she visited,
but the canvases continued to draw her attention.

At one—a portrait of a white sand sea—she stopped.

She stared... It wasn't that it was very familiar.
She seemed to want to test it, as shortly she reached out and touched its canvas.

----
At touch, the image rippled at her fingertips like touching a water reflection.
But, her fingers would not pass through.

...Nor a stone weight she hurled at the canvas, like a brute.

That gateway is only for the master of the house...

Grimacing and not knowing this, Maya moved on.

----
Through empty halls, where now and then the half-sight of gossamer phantoms stepping past
stopped her as they caught her eye...

Where a distant voice could sometimes be heard, shortly dissolving into silence
before she could even turn to guess at a source...

She wandered, seeking more, in a classical and eclectic world not "collected" from other places—but curated.

...Eventually she stole an empty journal. Eventually she stole a plume pen, seemingly full of infinite ink.

Sat on a pretty chair and by the unchanging gold and black light of the outside,
coming in from the many windows of the woman's manor...

Maya began to write.

----
// Maya, 1. // she wrote.

// When I first woke up, I was sure that I'd died, but I'm beginning to question the notion that I ever did.
Or, rather, I may more accurately be alive again.
Where I woke was a place called Arcaea, and Arcaea isn't where here is.
While Arcaea may be a cruel and strange place, I believe it is a world with more potential than this woman's.

And potential is the seed of hope—its blossom, the future. //

----
// My name seems to be Maya. I don't believe in God, // she wrote.

// For the one who might find this: I'll write down all that I know, for you. //
